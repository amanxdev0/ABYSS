Stop 1 · 0 m · Where it all begins

The Surface

Sunlight, waves, air. The ocean covers 71% of Earth's surface and holds 97% of all its water — and everything in the deep below starts right here, at the top.

By the numbers71% of Earth's surface97% of Earth's water1.4 billion km³

Stop 2 · 0–200 m · Epipelagic

The Sunlight Zone

Where 90% of ocean life lives. Light dies fast down here — red vanishes first, then orange, then yellow. By 200 metres, only blue remains, and photosynthesis becomes impossible.

ResidentsTunaSea turtlesDolphinsCoral reefs

Almost every fish you've ever heard of lives in this thin sunlit skin — just the top 200 of nearly 11,000 metres.

Stop 3 · 200–1,000 m · Mesopelagic

The Twilight Zone

The largest habitat on Earth by volume — and barely explored. Eyes grow enormous; many animals make their own light. Every night, trillions swim up to feed and sink back down — the largest migration on the planet, and it happens daily.

ResidentsLanternfishBristlemouthsJellies

Bristlemouths — tiny glowing fish — may be the most numerous vertebrates on Earth.

Stop 4 · 1,000–4,000 m · Bathypelagic

The Midnight Zone

No sunlight has ever reached here. The water is 4°C and the pressure crushes toward 400 atmospheres. Life cheats the darkness — three out of four animals down here can glow.

ResidentsAnglerfishGulper eelVampire squid

The anglerfish's famous lure glows with light from symbiotic bacteria — a living fishing rod.

Stop 5 · 4,000–6,000 m · Abyssal

The Abyss

A near-freezing desert plain covering half the planet's surface. Food arrives as marine snow — the slow rain of the dead from above. But around hydrothermal vents, entire cities of life run on chemistry instead of sunlight.

ResidentsTripod fishYeti crabTube worms

Black smokers blast water at 400°C — it never boils, because the pressure won't let it.

Stop 6 · 6,000–10,911 m · Hadal

The Trench

The Mariana Trench — a crescent gash 2,550 km long. At its bottom lies Challenger Deep, 10,911 metres down, under more than 1,000 atmospheres of pressure. Only a few dozen humans have ever touched the bottom.

Who went downTrieste · 1960Deepsea Challenger · 2012Limiting Factor · 2019

Jacques Piccard & Don Walsh first reached it in 1960; James Cameron went solo in 2012.
